CAUX already rules these out:

Trust accumulation, transparent pricing, no distractions at checkout.

The Central Consumer Protection Authority’s Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 list 13 specified dark patterns: false urgency, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, forced action, subscription trap, interface interference, bait and switch, drip pricing, disguised advertisement, nagging, trick question, SaaS billing and rogue malware. From 1 January 2027 the amended E-Commerce Rules make compliance and an annual self-audit mandatory, with the certificate displayed prominently. Many of these patterns are exactly what CAUX argues against on UX grounds: nagging and interference raise cognitive load, drip pricing breaks trust, basket sneaking and false urgency undermine confident decisions.
