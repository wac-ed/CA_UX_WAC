Digital Personal Data Protection Rules 2025, notified 13 November 2025 under the DPDP Act 2023. Phasing: Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 in force from notification; consent managers (Rule 4) from 13 November 2026; notice, security safeguards, breach handling, retention and children’s data (Rules 3, 5–16, 22–23) 18 months after notification, in May 2027 (sources differ between 13 and 14 May; confirm with legal). Notice (Rule 3): clear, independent and accessible; lists the personal data and specific purposes; explains how to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain. Consent (Act s.6): free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by clear affirmative action; withdrawal must be comparable in ease to giving consent. Children (Act s.9, Rule 10): verifiable parental consent, confirming the parent is an adult using reliable identity or age details or a virtual token such as DigiLocker; no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children. Erasure: delete data once the specified purpose is no longer served unless law requires retention; notify the user 48 hours before erasure; retain personal data and logs for at least one year. Breaches: inform affected users promptly with details and mitigation steps; give the Data Protection Board an initial intimation and a detailed report within 72 hours. Rights (Rule 9 onwards): publish the DPO or contact person’s details; give clear instructions for exercising rights; respond to grievances within 90 days. Security safeguards include encryption, masking or tokenisation, access controls, logging and backups. Penalty for failing to maintain reasonable security safeguards: up to ₹250 crore. Note: “reject as easy as accept” on cookie banners is an industry reading of these rules, not their wording.
